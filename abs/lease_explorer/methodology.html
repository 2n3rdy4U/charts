Methodology & Definitions

This page documents the data sources, field definitions, and analytical conventions behind every chart and table in the SEC Auto Lease ABS Data Explorer.

1. Data Source

All content is derived from SEC ABS-EE loan-level filings filed by auto-lease securitization trusts under Regulation AB II. These are monthly regulatory filings (~70 fields per lease per reporting period) disclosed to EDGAR. Our pipeline fetches the XML, parses it into per-issuer DuckDB files, dedups across filings, and aggregates here.

  • Issuers covered: 10 active auto-lease securitizers — American Honda (AHALT), BMW Vehicle Lease (BMWLT), Ford Credit (FCALT), GM Financial (GMALT), Hyundai (HALST), Mercedes-Benz (MBALT), Nissan (NALT), Toyota Lease (TMCLT), Volkswagen (VWALT), World Omni (WOALT).
  • Update cadence: The pipeline runs daily (SEC fetch at 6AM / 6PM ET). Typical latency from SEC filing to explorer is <24 hours.

2. Residual Value (RV) Methodology

RV is the dominant risk in lease ABS. When a lease terminates, the trust takes back the vehicle and sells it; if sale proceeds fall short of the originally-modeled residual, the trust realises a loss. We track both the static RV characteristics of each pool and the dynamic realized performance over time.
  • WA RV as % of vehicle value: SUM(baseResidualValue) / SUM(vehicleValueAmount), pool-weighted across active leases. Higher values = more RV exposure.
  • Residual source mix: SEC ABS-EE field baseResidualSourceCode — codes are 1 (Black Book), 2 (Automotive Lease Guide / ALG; industry standard for manufacturer-marked residuals), 98 (Other). An ALG-heavy pool typically carries conservative residuals and manufacturer backstop.
  • Cumulative realized gain/loss: For each terminated lease with both liquidationProceedsAmount and baseResidualValue populated, gain/loss = proceeds − residual. Summed over the life of the deal and signed (positive = pool recovered more than modeled).

3. Disposition / Termination Methodology

SEC ABS-EE field terminationIndicator records the reason a lease ended. Each asset's termination event is pulled from its final reporting row in the deduped database. Observed codes:

  • 1 — Matured (scheduled end-of-term return)
  • 2 — Early termination (purchase / payoff)
  • 3 — Early termination (default)
  • 4 — Repossession
  • 5 — Casualty / total loss
  • 6, other — Other / unspecified

Pool-level disposition mix is a health signal: a rising purchase-rate implies lessees perceive the vehicle as worth more than the contract residual (positive for the trust); a rising default-rate signals credit deterioration.

4. Delinquency Methodology

  • 60+ DPD rate: Leases with currentDelinquencyStatus ≥ 60 AND end-of-period balance > $10, divided by total active leases (balance > $10).
  • 30–59 DPD rate: 30 ≤ currentDelinquencyStatus ≤ 59, same denominator.
  • 30+ DPD rate: Combined 30–59 and 60+.

Lease DQ typically runs lower than loan DQ because lease pools are typically higher-FICO and lease terms are shorter.

5. Model Concentration

Top-3 vehicle models per series, ranked by asset count. Concentrated pools (>60% in three models) carry heightened exposure to model-specific resale shocks (e.g., a Consumer Reports downgrade or a recall affecting residual values on one nameplate).

6. Known Limitations

  • Some deals report vehicle-value at different points in the lifecycle (MSRP, cap cost, capitalized cost reduction net). Cross-issuer WA RV% comparisons should be treated as directional.
  • Termination codes beyond the 1–6 range are mapped to "Other" until observed in real data and documented here.
  • This explorer does not track deal-specific triggers (CNL thresholds, RV loss thresholds) written into individual indentures; those require prospectus-specific parsing.
