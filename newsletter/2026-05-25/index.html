The Week

May 18 – May 24

Bank-card AAAs print $1.75B as the prime/non-prime FICO gap collapses to a five-sigma low.

Chase ($1.25B, 2.97 WAL) and First National ($500M, 2.97 WAL) tag-teamed $1.75B of AAA credit-card paper this week, and the macro backdrop did the marketing for them: card charge-offs fell 62bp to 3.84% and 30+ DQs slipped 14bp to 2.92%. With the effective Fed funds rate down a basis point to 3.62% and 48-month auto rates dropping a chunky 35bp to 7.36%, the consumer-credit tape is printing the cleanest set of fundamentals we've seen all year. Issuers noticed. Two jumbo card prints in a single week is not a coincidence — it's a window.

Auto told a more textured story. Nissan A-3 cleared at I+44 and Mercedes A-3 at I+41 on near-identical 2.3-year WALs, a 3bp captive-vs-captive spread that looks like noise. Carvana A-3 came at I+68 on a 2.26 WAL — a 24–27bp pickup over the captives for what is structurally a deeper book. That premium has compressed meaningfully versus where Carvana paper traded a year ago, and it's hard to separate from the tape's other tell: the prime/non-prime FICO gap is 132 points, 5.2σ below the 24-month mean of 137.

The position: non-prime pools are quietly re-rating. A five-sigma narrowing in the credit-tier FICO gap isn't a measurement artifact — it's issuers tightening at the margin while prime pools sit at the ceiling. If you believe the loss-curve implication, the Carvana-style 25bp pickup over captive AAA is mispriced rich for the buyer, not the seller. Watch next week's deeper sub-prime auto shelves: if subordinate spreads compress faster than seniors, the tape is confirming what the FICO data is already saying. If they don't, the bank-card bid is doing all the work and the non-prime story is just optics.

“When the FICO gap moves five sigma, it's not the borrowers changing — it's the underwriters.” — as one ABS structurer framed it on a recent roadshow call
